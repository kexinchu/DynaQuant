\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models~\cite{shazeer2017outrageously} increase model capacity without increasing per-token computation proportionally: a router sends each token to only a small subset of expert subnetworks. Modern MoE language models, including Mixtral~\cite{jiang2024mixtral}, DeepSeek-V2~\cite{deepseekv2}, and Qwen3~\cite{qwen3technicalreport}, therefore contain tens to hundreds of billions of parameters while activating only a few billion per token. Sparse computation does not imply sparse storage, however. Unconstrained routing requires the full expert pool to remain addressable, and a single GPU must also accommodate non-expert weights, activations, the KV cache, and runtime workspaces. Qwen3-Next-80B alone occupies roughly 160\,GB at 16-bit precision. For 48--80\,GB accelerators, serving feasibility is consequently determined as much by expert residency as by arithmetic throughput.

Existing systems primarily trade capacity for either movement or fidelity. Expert offloading and prefetching~\cite{li2023adaptive,yi2023edgemoe,xue2024moe,eliseev2023fast,hwang2024pre,zhong2024adapmoe,tang2024hobbit,song2024promoe,shen2025expertflow} keep only part of the expert pool in HBM and fetch other experts through the host-device hierarchy. This approach is effective when a small, predictable working set leaves enough time to overlap transfers. Batched prefill and decode can activate a much larger fraction of the pool, however, increasing misses and exposed transfer time. Post-training quantization (PTQ)~\cite{lin2024awq,frantar2022gptq,kim2023mixturequantizedexpertsmoqe,duanmu2025mxmoe} takes the complementary approach: compress every expert sufficiently to remain resident. It removes routed cache misses, but the deployed precision map is normally fixed.

Fixing precision leaves an unexploited degree of freedom. Router traffic is heavy-tailed, but the identities of heavily used experts change across text, mathematical reasoning, and code (\autoref{fig:expert-active}). Under a fixed HBM budget, assigning the same fidelity to every expert can spend scarce bytes on experts that receive little current traffic, whereas an offline mixed-precision map can become stale after a workload shift. The opportunity is to retain a complete on-device expert set while changing \emph{which} experts receive a limited number of higher-precision slots.

This opportunity creates a systems problem beyond ranking expert importance. A precision change temporarily needs both old and new representations; concurrent changes can overrun HBM even when the final precision map fits. Dispatch must never observe partially copied weights, and reclamation must account for the interval between reading a pointer and completing the last kernel that uses it. Migration should also remain off the explicit forward-path synchronization chain, while noisy routing estimates must not trigger continual swaps. A practical runtime must satisfy these requirements without relying on a general-purpose allocator to recover from transient overcommitment.

We present \systemname, a single-GPU MoE runtime that treats expert precision as budgeted residency. \systemname\ keeps one executable version of every routed expert on the device and reallocates fixed higher-precision capacity according to online router statistics. Its policy combines selected routing probability mass, an exponential moving average, and hysteresis. Its mechanism uses partitioned fixed-block pools, byte-exact admission control, and a bounded staging pool. Precision versions are representation-immutable and accessed through versioned handles: a transition publishes a new handle only after its copy finishes, while leases and per-compute-stream last-use events protect the old block. Thus, scheduling, migration, publication, and reclamation have explicit safety boundaries.

\systemname\ targets the quality--throughput--memory tradeoff, not a claim that migration can outperform a fixed PTQ layout in raw latency. Static PTQ is the no-migration reference; the online policy spends controlled data movement to adapt fidelity as routing changes. The enforced memory invariant covers all \systemname-owned expert pools, staging blocks, and conversion workspaces. Whole-process safety additionally depends on deployment reservations for non-expert state, activations, and the KV cache, a boundary that the runtime checks at startup and the evaluation measures independently.

The main contributions of this work are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item We formulate fully resident MoE inference as online precision-residency allocation: the number of higher-precision slots is fixed by a measured HBM envelope, while their expert identities adapt to workload-dependent routing.
  \item We develop a routing-only policy with independently calibrated initialization, token-normalized online hotness, and hysteretic budget-feasible replacement. The policy changes expert identities without changing per-layer capacity.
  \item We design a safe replacement mechanism based on immutable versioned handles, read leases, CUDA last-use events, publish-after-copy transitions, partitioned resident pools, and admission-before-allocation. These mechanisms bound transient memory and prevent partially initialized or prematurely reclaimed representations from reaching dispatch.
  \item We implement the design for Qwen3-MoE, Qwen3-Next, and Phi-3.5-MoE and evaluate FP16/INT4 and INT4/INT2 tier pairs on one 48\,GB GPU. The study measures quality recovery, latency and throughput, process-level HBM use, component effects, and precision-budget sensitivity against fully resident PTQ and an executable offloading baseline where supported.
\end{itemize}
