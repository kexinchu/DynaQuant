\begin{abstract}
Large mixture-of-experts (MoE) models activate only a few experts per token, yet unconstrained routing requires every expert to remain available. On a single GPU, expert offloading can expose host-to-device transfers as batched routing expands the active set, whereas assigning one low precision to all experts keeps them resident but fixes their fidelity before deployment. \systemname\ instead reallocates a fixed number of higher-precision expert slots online. It estimates expert utility from router-selected probability mass, stabilizes decisions with hysteresis, and retains one executable representation of every routed expert on the GPU. Precision changes use precomputed host representations, fixed-block device pools, and admission control. Versioned immutable handles are published only after an asynchronous copy completes; read leases and per-stream CUDA events delay reclamation until no dispatch can reference the old block. The resulting invariant bounds resident expert, staging, and conversion-workspace memory, while declared reservations cover the remaining model state, activations, and KV cache. Across three MoE families and six workloads, \systemname\ recovers up to 79\% of the quality gap between the all-low and reference expert tiers. Experiments further characterize throughput, tail latency, memory compliance, component contributions, and sensitivity to the higher-precision budget.
\end{abstract}
