# C03 — Independent-knob conflict to shared feasibility

Create one 16:9 raster research-paper conceptual schematic. Reviewer takeaway: three locally reasonable demands conflict when they draw from one capacity, so feasibility must be resolved through one shared budget view. This is not a before/after performance result.

## Narrative and layout grammar

Use a left-to-right problem-to-resolution bridge without duplicating the full workflow. Left 28%: three compact demand motifs, labeled `Residency`, `Precision upgrade`, and `Prefetch staging`, converge toward one visibly congested but clean `bytes` merge; use line tension/competition, not red failure symbols or data. Center 28%: one large `Expert-memory budget` gate/capacity boundary. Right 30%: one `Coupled operating point` merge motif. Place `memory bound` and `quality constraint` as small gate tags around the center-to-right connector. This must be one continuous process, not two cloned “before” and “after” pipelines.

## Non-visual graph contract

Internal IDs must not render: N_BUDGET, N_RESIDENCY, N_PRECISION, N_PREFETCH, N_POINT, N_MEMORY_BOUND, N_QUALITY_BOUND; E_ALLOC_R/P/F, E_R_OUT, E_P_OUT, E_F_OUT, E_MEM_GATE, E_QUAL_GATE.

Visual projection: reverse the spatial presentation without reversing semantic direction. The shared budget remains the source of allowed allocation conceptually; show the three demand motifs pressing against/meeting the one budget boundary by non-directional grouping strokes, then use the contracted allocation/consequence relations to the coupled point. Do not draw arrows from the uses into the budget if that would imply returned data. The only directed consequence lines are from each use motif toward the coupled point, bundled after the central gate and labeled with their distinct outcomes.

## Visible text and motifs

Node labels: `Expert-memory budget`, `Residency`, `Precision upgrade`, `Prefetch staging`, `Coupled operating point`. Line labels: `bytes`, `fewer avoidable misses`, `potential quality benefit`, `timely arrival / overlap`. Tags: `memory bound`, `quality constraint`.

Use resident tiles, one narrow-to-wide expert representation, an incoming expert/deadline marker, one bounded capacity gate, and one selector/merge. No bullets.

## Hard constraints

Do not make two full pipelines. Do not label regions “bad”, “good”, “before”, “after”, “baseline”, or “ours”. No performance arrows, charts, axes, curves, heatmaps, numeric results, percentages, speedups, error bars, or synthetic data. No claim that the coupled point is better or optimal; it is only feasibility coordination. No uncontracted arrows, no internal IDs, no hotness-to-quality arrow.

Surface: formal academic line-art schematic, restrained warning accent only at the shared capacity boundary, otherwise neutral scientific palette and generous whitespace.
