The motivation sequence must answer one question: **why can precision, residency, and prefetch not be tuned as independent knobs under one GPU expert-memory budget?**

## Recommended motivation sequence

1. Use the optional conceptual teaser only to establish the shared-byte conflict.
2. Use O1 to replace “activation density implies stalls” with a measured causal chain: miss bytes versus per-deadline overlap capacity versus exposed wait.
3. Use O2 immediately after O1; this is the proof that the merged research question exists.

5. Move reused `expert-active` and `ppl` panels to Background or make them compact O3 support; do not let them compete visually with O1--O3.

## Paper-core semantics lock for the conceptual teaser

Required concepts:

- one bounded expert-memory budget;
- three competing byte uses: low-precision residency, precision upgrade, prefetch staging;
- three distinct consequences: avoidable misses, potential quality benefit, and timely transfer overlap;
- memory feasibility and quality constraint remain explicit boundaries;
- coupling, not measured superiority, is the takeaway.

Forbidden claims/content:

- no numerical allocation proportions;
- no “optimal”, “best”, speedup, latency reduction, accuracy gain, or quality-recovery percentage;
- no plot axes, line charts, heatmaps, colored response surfaces, error bars, or data-point markers;
- no claim that hot experts are quality-sensitive;
- no claim that the joint online controller has already outperformed baselines;
- no duplicated full workflows for the three byte uses;
- no independent full budget owned by multiple controllers.

## Common semantic graph

Primary visible modules/containers:

1. `Expert-memory budget` — bounded shared resource.
2. `Residency` — keep low-precision experts present.
3. `Precision upgrade` — spend extra bytes on a higher-precision representation.
4. `Prefetch staging` — reserve bytes early for upcoming experts.
5. `Coupled operating point` — a conceptual convergence/decision target, not a measured result.

Line-carried terms: `bytes`, `fewer avoidable misses`, `potential quality benefit`, `timely arrival / overlap`, `memory bound`, and `quality constraint`. These must be edge/port/tag labels, not peer boxes.

Allowed connector families:

| Family | Source → target | Semantics | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| CF1 | Expert-memory budget → three byte uses | bounded byte allocation, one fork/bus | S0 report; merged draft §2/§3.2 |
| CF2 | Residency → coupled operating point | fewer avoidable misses | S0 report; merged draft §2/§3.2 |
| CF3 | Precision upgrade → coupled operating point | potential quality benefit and larger representation | S0 report; DynaExQ quota/quality evidence boundary |
| CF4 | Prefetch staging → coupled operating point | timely arrival/overlap opportunity | S0 report; DynaMoE horizon mechanism |
| CF5 | Memory bound / quality constraint → coupled operating point | feasibility gates, not data flow | merged objective and S0 report |

No reverse arrows, decorative arrows, cross-links among the three uses, or arrows from hotness directly to quality are allowed.

## Layout and density limits

- 16:9 exploratory canvas, suitable for later double-column adaptation.
- Maximum five large labeled modules/containers.
- Exactly one canonical conceptual process; repeated experts are at most three small unlabeled exemplar glyphs.
- Maximum five visible connector families; CF1 is one fork/bus, not three unrelated arrows.
- Short visible labels only; all caveats stay in caption/manuscript.
- Background/context area at most 20%; primary conceptual mechanism at least 65%.
- No separate legend unless the candidate uses line-style semantics that cannot be read directly.
