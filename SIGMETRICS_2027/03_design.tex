\section{Design}
\label{sec:design}

\subsection{Overview}
\label{sec:model}

\systemname\ answers the allocation question with one admission procedure, shown in Figure~\ref{fig:arch}.
The procedure chooses each expert's representation, residency, and transfer issue time, then commits those choices as one exchange.

  \caption{\systemname\ architecture. The State Monitor builds one snapshot (step~1); the Lease Generator emits fidelity, residency, and lookahead requests (step~2); the valuator scores a donor-closed exchange with one deadline replay (step~3); and the Exchange Coordinator admits it while both accounts hold (step~4). The Safe Executor commits the schedule on one host-to-device queue and one expert arena (step~5).}
  \label{fig:arch}

The State Monitor builds one snapshot of routing demand, deadlines, quality risk, bandwidth, and byte state (step~1).
The Lease Generator emits fidelity, residency, and lookahead requests inside the candidate envelope \(S_t\) (step~2).
The coordinator attaches donor leases, and the valuator replays the migration queue before and after the resulting exchange (step~3).
The coordinator admits the exchange when predicted exposed wait falls and both the quality-risk and byte accounts hold (step~4).
The Safe Executor reserves, copies, publishes, and reclaims according to the admitted schedule (step~5).
The return path updates the next snapshot and \(S_t\).

The object passed from step~4 to step~5 is
\begin{equation}
  x=(Z^{+},D,\mathcal{T}),
  \label{eq:exchange}
\end{equation}
where \(Z^{+}\) denotes the requested leases, \(D\) the donor leases released to fund them, and \(\mathcal{T}\) the transition schedule.
The coordinator scores the state after the whole exchange, so the value of one use includes its effect on the other two.
Peak expert memory tests whether the transition fits.
Quality risk tests the admission proxy.

A runtime policy \(\pi\) chooses the representation, resident set, and transfer issue time of every expert.
The budget \(B\) is the expert memory remaining after non-expert weights, the KV cache, activations, and fixed workspaces are reserved, and \(C\) is the measured host-to-device copy rate defined in Section~\ref{sec:serving}.
The allocation problem is
\begin{equation}
  \min_{\pi}\; \operatorname{P95}(\mathrm{TPOT};\pi)
  \quad \text{subject to} \quad
  M_{\mathrm{peak}}(\pi) \le B,
  \qquad
  \Delta Q(\pi) \le \epsilon.
  \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
\systemname\ treats Eq.~\eqref{eq:objective} as that problem.
The executor enforces \(M_{\mathrm{peak}}(\pi)\le B\) on every transition.
The controller admits with \(\widehat R(X)\le\widehat\epsilon\) in place of the \(\Delta Q(\pi)\le\epsilon\) test.
The deadline replay charges \(C\) for the migration-queue time occupied by each exchange.
The runtime consults a calibrated risk estimate while making decisions; Section~\ref{sec:quality-boundary} explains why this estimate is not a per-request quality guarantee.

An expert is absent, resident at low precision, resident at high precision, or in transition toward a resident state.
Its packed footprints are \(m_i^{\mathrm{lo}}\) and \(m_i^{\mathrm{hi}}\).
A high-precision resident accounts for a low-precision residency plus the fidelity increment \(\delta m_i^{\mathrm{fid}}=m_i^{\mathrm{hi}}-m_i^{\mathrm{lo}}\), while remaining one packed block.
Lookahead copies an absent expert before its deadline and becomes residency at the selected tier once the copy is published.
The low- and high-precision loads of one absent expert are mutually exclusive.
Section~\ref{sec:admit} specifies when a reserved representation may be published and when donor bytes may be reclaimed.

Figure~\ref{fig:exchange} traces one exchange through both budgets.
Lookahead \(e_4\) does not fit in the one free unit, and \(e_1\)'s residency still leaves it short, so the coordinator also releases \(e_2\)'s fidelity increment.
The replay admits the exchange only when \(e_4\)'s saved wait exceeds the cost of \(e_1\)'s later reload and \(e_2\)'s low-precision copy, while the added quality risk remains within the proxy limit.

\subsection{State Monitor}
\label{sec:monitor}

The State Monitor performs step~1 in Figure~\ref{fig:arch}: it turns observed runtime facts into one immutable snapshot.
It neither predicts future uses nor constructs or admits an exchange.
The snapshot contains demand and timing, quality, and execution and byte state.

\paragraph{Demand and timing.}
The monitor records recent routing transitions, per-layer expert-kernel time, and pre-gating logits when the checkpoint exposes them.
Section~\ref{sec:leases} converts these observations into future-use probabilities, deadlines, and reuse estimates.

\paragraph{Quality.}
An offline sensitivity \(q_i\geq 0\) is the increase in calibration loss when only expert \(i\) uses its low representation.
The runtime does not modify \(q_i\).
After each forward step, the monitor updates \(w_i\) with an exponential average of expert \(e\)'s router-weighted dispatch frequency.
The product \(w_iq_i\) estimates how often the current workload exercises that sensitivity.
For a planned state \(X\), \(h_i(X)=1\) when expert \(i\)'s next execution uses the high representation.
The monitor provides
\begin{equation}
  \widehat R(X)
  =\sum_i w_i q_i\bigl(1-h_i(X)\bigr).
  \label{eq:risk}
\end{equation}
The coordinator admits against \(\widehat R(X)\leq\widehat\epsilon\).
It does not convert risk into milliseconds.

\paragraph{Execution and byte state.}
The monitor separates the published state used by dispatch from the reserved bytes of an accepted transition, and reports their locations and byte counts separately.
It also records measured bandwidth, exposed wait, late bytes, unused lookahead bytes, and eviction-induced reload bytes.
Stall \(s_t\) is the fraction of routed-layer time spent waiting for expert transfers.
Pollution \(p_t\) is unused lookahead bytes plus reload bytes, divided by the lookahead traffic admitted in that period.
The monitor reports the exponentially smoothed values \(\widetilde s_t\) and \(\widetilde p_t\).
Section~\ref{sec:coordinator} uses them to change \(S_t\); the monitor does not.

The resulting snapshot is the only interface from the monitor to the Lease Generator.

\subsection{Lease Generator and Valuator}
\label{sec:leases}
\label{sec:actions}

The Lease Generator performs step~2 in Figure~\ref{fig:arch}: it turns the
monitor snapshot into lease requests.  The Valuator performs step~3, but only
after the coordinator has attached donors and thereby closed a request into
an exchange.  Neither component admits an exchange.

\paragraph{Bound the candidate set.}
The adaptive envelope \(S_t\) is the maximum routed-layer depth from which the generator may produce requests.
It limits candidate generation only and reserves no memory, bandwidth, or lookahead quota.
Section~\ref{sec:coordinator} adjusts \(S_t\) from observed stall and pollution.
For a resident set \(R\) and predicted expert set \(\hat U(S)\), the generator retains a prefix depth only when
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{i\in\hat U(S)\setminus R}m_i\le S T_{\ell}C.
  \label{eq:overlap}
\end{equation}
This comparison filters candidates.
The subsequent replay still uses individual issue times and deadlines.

\paragraph{Forecast uses and form requests.}
For each retained layer, the forecaster reads pre-gating logits from the snapshot when they are available and otherwise uses recent routing transitions.
It produces a predicted use \(j=(i_j,b_j,\omega_j,a_j,d_j)\): expert, representation tier, occurrence probability, earliest issue time, and deadline.
The deadline is the latest ready time that introduces no transfer wait.

A lease \(z=(u,i,b,[t_s,t_f))\) records its use \(u\in\{\mathrm{fid},\mathrm{res},\mathrm{look}\}\), expert, bytes, and expected lifetime.
The interval guides valuation: an early use may shorten a lookahead lease, whereas a late copy retains its bytes until completion or cancellation.
Table~\ref{tab:lease-fields} shows how all three requests enter the same two accounts and migration-queue replay.

A fidelity request claims \(\delta m_i^{\mathrm{fid}}\) beyond the base residency lease and lasts for a slow period.
A demotion releases that increment only after the low representation is published.
A residency request renews \(m_i^{\mathrm{lo}}\) for one fast period; eviction releases it after outstanding readers finish.
For an absent expert, the generator emits mutually exclusive low- and high-tier lookahead requests when their resulting risk is feasible.
A lookahead lease lasts from issue until use, after which it becomes residency at the selected tier or is reclaimed.

\paragraph{Value a closed exchange.}
The generator sends requests, not scores, to the coordinator.
For each request, the coordinator proposes one or more donor sets \(D\) and calls the valuator on the resulting exchange \(x=(Z^+,D,\mathcal T)\).
The valuator replays transfers in earliest-deadline-first order.
A resident expert is ready at the start of the window.
An absent expert's completion time includes bytes already queued and its earliest issue time.
Uses of the same expert and tier share one readiness event under continuous batching.
The predicted exposed wait is
\begin{equation}
  \widehat L(X)
  =\sum_j \omega_j\,[r_j(X)-d_j]_+.
  \label{eq:predicted-wait}
\end{equation}
This scheduling surrogate is calibrated against measured tail latency.
It is not a direct estimate of the P95 quantile in Eq.~\eqref{eq:objective}.

The valuator returns
\begin{equation}
  G(x\mid X)
  =\widehat L(X)-\widehat L(X\oplus x)-C_{\mathrm{move}}(x),
  \label{eq:value}
\end{equation}
where \(X\oplus x\) applies the request, donors, and transition schedule, and \(C_{\mathrm{move}}\) is exposed transition cost.
The same function scores all three uses: fidelity changes representation and transfer size, residency removes a transfer, and lookahead changes its issue time.
One replay prevents a retained expert and its lookahead from both receiving credit for the same avoided transfer, and it charges every released donor for its later reload.

\subsection{Exchange Coordinator}
\label{sec:coordinator}
\label{sec:alg}

The Exchange Coordinator attaches donors before valuation and makes the only admission decision.
One control event has three stages: close a request, value and admit the closed exchange, and refresh the next candidate set.

\paragraph{Close.}
The coordinator initializes provisional state \(X\) from the monitor snapshot.
Slack, \(B-M_{\mathrm{live}}-M_{\mathrm{reserved}}\), funds a request directly when it is sufficient.
Otherwise \(D\) is built from fidelity leases eligible for demotion, residency leases eligible for eviction, and lookahead leases eligible for cancellation.
Reclaimable leases come first: a copy that has not started or is no longer useful, and a resident whose predicted reuse lies beyond \(S_t\).
A resident that is still needed can become a donor only when the replay charges its later reload.
Donor construction scans leases in increasing counterfactual loss, the extra predicted wait and move cost caused by releasing the lease.
In Figure~\ref{fig:exchange}, lookahead \(e_4\) is closed with \(e_1\)'s residency lease and \(e_2\)'s fidelity increment.

A closed exchange is feasible only when the request and its donors do not conflict, its resulting risk remains within \(\widehat\epsilon\), and its complete transition satisfies
\begin{equation}
  M_{\mathrm{live}}(t)+M_{\mathrm{reserved}}(t)\le B
  \qquad \forall t.
  \label{eq:fit}
\end{equation}
A release becomes byte credit only at its scheduled reclaim time.
Two exchanges therefore cannot spend the same donor, and future bytes cannot be spent as current slack.

If the published state exceeds the risk account, the coordinator first restores feasibility through promotion exchanges with the smallest replayed latency cost per unit of risk removed.
This repair uses the same closure and valuation path.
Once the state is feasible, a demotion can fund another request only when the complete resulting exchange remains feasible.

\paragraph{Value and admit.}
Let \(b_x^{\mathrm{req}}\) be the requested lease's bytes over its next lifetime, including bytes renewed by a residency request.
The coordinator ranks feasible exchanges with positive \(G\) by
\begin{equation}
  \eta(x\mid X)=G(x\mid X)/b_x^{\mathrm{req}}.
  \label{eq:density}
\end{equation}
After each admission it updates both provisional accounts, invalidates conflicting requests, and asks the valuator to replay the affected exchanges.
Retaining an expert can remove its lookahead request, and changing a representation can alter several completion times.
The search stops when no feasible exchange has positive \(G\).
Eq.~\eqref{eq:objective} is the offline problem, and \textsc{Static-Joint} solves it once from calibration statistics. Each control event admits the feasible exchange with the largest \(\eta=G/b_x^{\mathrm{req}}\), where \(G\) is the drop in predicted exposed wait after the donor-closed replay. Because \(G\) depends on exchanges already admitted, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:objective} is a tail over requests, \(\eta\) is a snapshot heuristic. Admission still requires \(\widehat R\le\widehat\epsilon\).

\paragraph{Refresh.}
Every \(T_{\mathrm{fast}}\) routed layers, the generator refreshes residency and lookahead requests.
Every \(T_{\mathrm{slow}}\) forward steps, it adds fidelity requests to the same pool.
Minimum fidelity tenure and replacement hysteresis suppress changes too short to repay their copies.
At a fast event, the coordinator increases \(S_t\) by one layer when \(\widetilde s_t-\lambda\widetilde p_t>\theta_h\), decreases it by one layer when the difference falls below \(-\theta_h\), and leaves it unchanged otherwise.
This changes only which future experts the generator may propose.
Donor closure, replay, and \(\eta\) still determine admission.

\begin{algorithm}[tbh]
\caption{One \systemname\ control event.}
\label{alg:period}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require snapshot, published and reserved leases, envelope \(S_t\)
\If{\(\widetilde s_t-\lambda\widetilde p_t>\theta_h\)}
  \State \(S_t\gets\min(S_t+1,S_{\max})\)
\ElsIf{\(\widetilde s_t-\lambda\widetilde p_t<-\theta_h\)}
  \State \(S_t\gets\max(S_t-1,S_{\min})\)
\EndIf
\State \(Z\gets\) residency and lookahead requests with depth \(\le S_t\)
\If{this event is a slow boundary}
  \State add fidelity requests to \(Z\)
\EndIf
\If{\(\widehat R(X)>\widehat\epsilon\)}
  \State close promotions of minimum replayed cost per unit risk
  \State admit them until \(\widehat R(X)\le\widehat\epsilon\)
\EndIf
\ForAll{request \(z\in Z\)}
  \State attach donors \(D\) in increasing counterfactual loss
  \State close \(x=(z,D,\mathcal T)\)
  \If{\(x\) violates \(\widehat R\), Eq.~\eqref{eq:fit}, or deadlines}
    \State discard \(x\)
  \Else
    \State compute \(G(x\mid X)\) and \(\eta(x\mid X)\) by deadline replay
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\While{some feasible \(x\) has \(G(x\mid X)>0\)}
  \State admit \(x^\star\gets\arg\max_x\eta(x\mid X)\)
  \State apply \(x^\star\) to provisional \(X\) and both accounts
  \State drop conflicting exchanges and replay the rest
\EndWhile
\State submit the admitted schedules \((Z^+,D,\mathcal T)\)
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The coordinator's only output is this set of admitted, donor-closed schedules.

\subsection{Safe Executor}
\label{sec:admit}

The Safe Executor performs step~5 in Figure~\ref{fig:arch}.
Its input is an admitted schedule \((Z^+,D,\mathcal T)\).
Its output is a new published state and telemetry for the next snapshot.
It does not choose among fidelity, residency, and lookahead.

\paragraph{Reserve.}
The executor reserves every extent in \(\mathcal T\) in one transaction and checks the transition peak in Eq.~\eqref{eq:fit}.
A destination that must exist before its donor becomes reclaimable is bound to an actual extent during this transaction.
If no compatible extent can be bound, or the peak exceeds \(B\), the executor discards the schedule and leaves the published state unchanged.

\paragraph{Copy and publish.}
\label{sec:publish}
Copies run on the migration stream.
A demotion keeps serving the high representation until the low replacement is published.
An eviction-only donor is reclaimed after its readers finish.
The executor atomically publishes the new handle only after the destination copy completes.
Until then the forward path continues to use the old representation.

\paragraph{Reclaim and ready work.}
The executor reclaims an old extent only after its reader count reaches zero and its recorded uses complete.
After routing, groups with published handles issue before groups waiting on a transfer.
A missed lookahead waits only in its assigned group, and routing and top-\(k\) do not change.
A speculative exchange may displace a protected resident only when its donor-closed replay has already charged that resident's reload.

\paragraph{Telemetry.}
The executor returns the measurements defined in Section~\ref{sec:monitor}.
They update the next snapshot and do not roll back a published representation.
This closes the loop from snapshot to request, closed exchange, admitted schedule, and telemetry.
