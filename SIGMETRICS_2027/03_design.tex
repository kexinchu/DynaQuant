\section{Design}
\label{sec:design}

\subsection{Overview}
\label{sec:model}

\systemname\ answers the allocation question with one admission procedure, shown in Figure~\ref{fig:arch}.
The procedure chooses each expert's precision, whether it stays on the GPU, and when its copy starts, then commits those choices together: the copies being requested, the experts demoted or evicted to free their bytes, and the transfer schedule.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \input{figures/dynabyte_overview.tex}
  \caption{Admission path. Numbered arrows are the five steps in this section.}
  \label{fig:arch}
\end{figure}

The State Monitor records routing demand, deadlines, \(\widehat R\), bandwidth, and which bytes are on the GPU (step~1).
The generator proposes high-precision copies, resident experts, and early copies within the next \(S_t\) layers (step~2).
The coordinator chooses which resident experts to demote or evict to free those bytes, and the valuator replays the copy queue before and after that choice (step~3).
The coordinator admits the choice when predicted exposed wait falls and \(\widehat R\le\widehat\epsilon/\kappa\) (step~4).
The executor reserves, copies, and reclaims on that schedule, keeps the byte peak within \(B\), and publishes the new handle only when the side probe measures \(\Delta\mathrm{PPL}\le\epsilon\) (step~5).
The return path updates the next snapshot and \(S_t\).

The object passed from step~4 to step~5 is
\begin{equation}
  x=(Z^{+},D,\mathcal{T}),
  \label{eq:exchange}
\end{equation}
The coordinator scores the GPU state after the demotions, evictions, and new copies have all been applied. The replay charges the shared queue for a demoted or evicted expert's later reload and does not credit one avoided transfer twice. Scoring the new copy's transfer time minus the released expert's transfer time prices the same two copies without that replay.

A policy \(\pi\) assigns every expert a representation, a residency decision, and a transfer issue time.
The allocation problem is
\begin{equation}
  \min_{\pi}\; \operatorname{P95}(\mathrm{TPOT};\pi)
  \quad \text{subject to} \quad
  M_{\mathrm{peak}}(\pi) \le B,
  \qquad
  \Delta Q(\pi) \le \epsilon.
  \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:objective} is the offline problem reported in Section~\ref{sec:eval}.
The executor enforces \(M_{\mathrm{peak}}(\pi)\le B\) on every transition.
One control step does not observe the request-level p95.
Admission does not read measured \(\Delta\mathrm{PPL}\).
It admits the copies, demotions, and evictions when predicted exposed wait falls and \(\widehat R\le\widehat\epsilon/\kappa\).
Section~\ref{sec:impl} sets \(\kappa>1\) to the maximum of \((\Delta\mathrm{PPL}/\epsilon)/(\widehat R/\widehat\epsilon)\) on the calibration prompts, rounded up to the next hundredth.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:period} probes the candidate after its extents are reserved inside \(B\) and copied on the serving GPU. The previous handles keep serving until that probe measures \(\Delta\mathrm{PPL}\le\epsilon\).
The state is published only when that reading is within \(\epsilon\).
If it exceeds \(\epsilon\), the last admitted exchange is rolled back and promotions run until the probe is within \(\epsilon\) or no promotion fits in \(B\).
Section~\ref{sec:eval-models} checks the Qwen3-30B states in Figure~\ref{fig:eval-validation}(b).
The deadline replay charges \(C\) for the migration-queue time occupied by each exchange.

An expert is absent, resident in the low-precision representation, resident in the high-precision representation, or in transition toward a resident state.
Its packed footprints are \(m_i^{\mathrm{lo}}\) and \(m_i^{\mathrm{hi}}\).
A resident high-precision representation is the packed low-precision representation plus the fidelity increment \(\delta m_i^{\mathrm{fid}}\) in Table~\ref{tab:glossary}, and it remains one packed block.
Once its copy is published, a lookahead lease becomes residency at the selected tier.
The low- and high-precision representations of one absent expert are mutually exclusive.
Section~\ref{sec:admit} specifies when a reserved representation may be published and when donor bytes may be reclaimed.

Figure~\ref{fig:exchange} traces one exchange through both budgets.
Lookahead \(e_4\) does not fit in the one free unit, and \(e_1\)'s residency still leaves it short, so the coordinator also releases \(e_2\)'s fidelity increment.
The replay admits the exchange only when \(e_4\)'s saved wait exceeds the cost of \(e_1\)'s later reload and \(e_2\)'s low-precision copy, while the added \(\widehat R\) remains within \(\widehat\epsilon/\kappa\).

\subsection{State Monitor}
\label{sec:monitor}

The State Monitor performs step~1 in Figure~\ref{fig:arch}: it turns observed runtime facts into one immutable snapshot.
The snapshot contains demand and timing, quality, and execution and byte state.

\paragraph{Demand and timing.}
The monitor records recent routing transitions, per-layer expert-kernel time, and pre-gating logits when the checkpoint exposes them.
Section~\ref{sec:leases} converts these observations into future-use probabilities, deadlines, and reuse estimates.

\paragraph{Quality.}
An offline sensitivity \(q_i\geq 0\) is the increase in calibration loss when only expert \(i\) uses its low representation.
The runtime does not modify \(q_i\).
After each forward step, the monitor updates \(w_i\) with an exponential average of expert \(e\)'s router-weighted dispatch frequency.
The product \(w_iq_i\) estimates how often the current workload exercises that sensitivity.
For a planned state \(X\), \(h_i(X)=1\) when expert \(i\)'s next execution uses the high representation.
The monitor provides
\begin{equation}
  \widehat R(X)
  =\sum_i w_i q_i\bigl(1-h_i(X)\bigr).
  \label{eq:risk}
\end{equation}
The coordinator admits against \(\widehat R(X)\leq\widehat\epsilon/\kappa\).

\paragraph{Execution and byte state.}
The monitor separates the published state used by dispatch from the reserved bytes of an accepted transition, and reports their locations and byte counts separately.
It also records measured bandwidth, exposed wait, late bytes, unused lookahead bytes, and eviction-induced reload bytes.
Stall \(s_t\) is the fraction of routed-layer time spent waiting for expert transfers.
Pollution \(p_t\) is unused lookahead bytes plus reload bytes, divided by the lookahead traffic admitted in that period.
The monitor reports the exponentially smoothed values \(\widetilde s_t\) and \(\widetilde p_t\).
Section~\ref{sec:coordinator} changes \(S_t\) from the prefetch hit rate. Section~\ref{sec:coordinator} admits the exchange.

The resulting snapshot is the only interface from the monitor to the Lease Generator.

\subsection{Which Copies Are Proposed}
\label{sec:leases}
\label{sec:actions}

The generator performs step~2 in Figure~\ref{fig:arch}: it turns the monitor snapshot into the copies proposed for admission.
The valuator performs step~3 only after the coordinator has attached the experts to demote or evict, closing that proposal into one exchange \(x=(Z^{+},D,\mathcal{T})\).

\paragraph{Bound the candidate set.}
The envelope \(S_t\) is the maximum routed-layer depth for candidate generation and allocates no bytes (Table~\ref{tab:glossary}).
Section~\ref{sec:coordinator} adjusts \(S_t\) from the prefetch hit rate.
For a resident set \(R\) and predicted expert set \(\hat U(S)\), the generator retains a prefix depth only when
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{i\in\hat U(S)\setminus R}m_i\le S T_{\ell}C.
  \label{eq:overlap}
\end{equation}
This comparison filters candidates.
The subsequent replay still uses individual issue times and deadlines.

\paragraph{Forecast uses and form leases.}
For each retained layer, the forecaster reads pre-gating logits from the snapshot when they are available and otherwise uses recent routing transitions.
It produces a predicted use \(j=(i_j,b_j,\omega_j,a_j,d_j)\): expert, representation tier, occurrence probability, earliest issue time, and deadline.
The deadline is the latest ready time that introduces no transfer wait.

A lease is the record in Table~\ref{tab:glossary}.
The interval guides valuation: an early use may shorten a lookahead lease, whereas a late copy retains its bytes until completion or cancellation.
Table~\ref{tab:lease-fields} shows how all three leases enter the byte account, \(\widehat R\) (Eq.~\eqref{eq:risk}), and migration-queue replay.

A fidelity lease claims \(\delta m_i^{\mathrm{fid}}\) beyond the base residency lease and lasts for \(T_{\mathrm{slow}}\) forward steps.
A demotion releases that increment only after the low representation is published.
A residency lease renews \(m_i^{\mathrm{lo}}\) for \(T_{\mathrm{fast}}\) routed layers; eviction releases it after outstanding readers finish.
For an absent expert, the generator emits mutually exclusive low- and high-tier lookahead leases when their resulting \(\widehat R\) is feasible.
A lookahead lease lasts from issue until use, after which it becomes residency at the selected tier or is reclaimed.

\paragraph{Value a closed exchange.}
The generator sends leases, not scores, to the coordinator.
For each lease, the coordinator proposes one or more donor sets \(D\) and calls the valuator on the resulting exchange \(x=(Z^+,D,\mathcal T)\).
The valuator replays transfers in earliest-deadline-first order.
A resident expert is ready at the start of the window.
An absent expert's completion time includes bytes already queued and its earliest issue time.
Uses of the same expert and tier share one readiness event under continuous batching.
The predicted exposed wait is
\begin{equation}
  \widehat L(X)
  =\sum_j \omega_j\,[r_j(X)-d_j]_+.
  \label{eq:predicted-wait}
\end{equation}
\(\widehat L\) sums probability-weighted exposed wait inside one replay window; Eq.~\eqref{eq:objective} is the request-level p95.
Section~\ref{sec:eval-models} checks both.

The valuator returns
\begin{equation}
  G(x\mid X)
  =\widehat L(X)-\widehat L(X\oplus x)-C_{\mathrm{move}}(x),
  \label{eq:value}
\end{equation}
where \(X\oplus x\) applies the lease, donors, and transition schedule, and \(C_{\mathrm{move}}\) is exposed transition cost.
One replay prevents a retained expert and its lookahead from both receiving credit for the same avoided transfer, and it charges every released donor for its later reload.

\subsection{Which Copies Are Admitted}
\label{sec:coordinator}
\label{sec:alg}

The coordinator decides which experts to release before it makes the only admission decision.
One control step has three stages: attach the experts to release, admit by \(G\), and refresh the next candidate set.

\paragraph{Close.}
The coordinator initializes provisional state \(X\) from the monitor snapshot.
Free bytes, \(B-M_{\mathrm{published}}-M_{\mathrm{reserved}}\), pay for the requested copy when they are sufficient.
Otherwise \(D\) is built from high-precision copies that can be demoted, resident experts that can be evicted, and early copies that can be cancelled.
Copies that have not started, copies that are no longer useful, and residents whose predicted reuse lies beyond \(S_t\) are released first.
A resident that is still needed is released only when the replay charges its later reload.
The scan follows increasing counterfactual loss, the extra predicted wait and move cost caused by the release.
In Figure~\ref{fig:exchange}, the early copy of \(e_4\) is funded by releasing \(e_1\)'s residency and the extra high-precision bytes of \(e_2\).

A closed exchange is feasible only when the lease and its donors do not conflict, its resulting \(\widehat R\) remains within \(\widehat\epsilon/\kappa\), and its complete transition satisfies
\begin{equation}
  M_{\mathrm{published}}(t)+M_{\mathrm{reserved}}(t)\le B
  \qquad \forall t.
  \label{eq:fit}
\end{equation}
A release enters the byte account only at its scheduled reclaim time, so the same bytes cannot pay for two copies, and bytes that become free only later cannot be spent as free bytes now.

If \(\widehat R\) of the published state exceeds \(\widehat\epsilon/\kappa\), the coordinator first restores feasibility through promotion exchanges with the smallest replayed latency cost per unit of \(\widehat R\) removed.
This repair uses the same close and valuation steps.
Once the state is feasible, a demotion can fund another lease only when the complete resulting exchange remains feasible.

\paragraph{Value and admit.}
Let \(b_x^{\mathrm{req}}\) be the requested lease's bytes over its next lifetime, including bytes renewed by a residency lease.
The coordinator ranks feasible exchanges with positive \(G\) by
\begin{equation}
  \eta(x\mid X)=G(x\mid X)/b_x^{\mathrm{req}}.
  \label{eq:density}
\end{equation}
After each admission it updates both provisional accounts, invalidates conflicting requests, and asks the valuator to replay the affected exchanges.
Retaining an expert can remove its lookahead lease, and changing a representation can alter several completion times.
The search stops when no feasible exchange has positive \(G\).
Each control event admits the feasible exchange with the largest \(\eta=G/b_x^{\mathrm{req}}\), where \(G\) is the drop in predicted exposed wait after the replay of the closed exchange. \(G\) depends on exchanges already admitted. \(\eta\) ranks exchanges by the drop in weighted exposed wait inside the current window, per requested byte. Section~\ref{sec:eval-models} reports when that drop moves window p95. Removing the admissions that raise window p95 is computed after the run and is not the admission rule. Section~\ref{sec:eval-setup} freezes one calibration assignment as \textsc{Static-Joint}. Admission still requires \(\widehat R\le\widehat\epsilon/\kappa\).

\paragraph{Refresh.}
Every \(T_{\mathrm{fast}}\) routed layers, the generator refreshes residency and lookahead leases.
Every \(T_{\mathrm{slow}}\) forward steps, it adds fidelity leases to the same set.
Minimum fidelity tenure and replacement hysteresis suppress changes too short to repay their copies.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:period} adjusts \(S_t\), and Section~\ref{sec:impl} states the Qwen3-30B constants.
Refresh changes the candidate set.
Admission remains closure, replay, and \(\eta\).

\begin{algorithm}[tbh]
\caption{One \systemname\ control event.}
\label{alg:period}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require snapshot, published and reserved leases, envelope \(S_t\)
\If{prefetch hit rate \(< 0.25\)}
  \State \(S_t\gets\max(S_t-1,S_{\min})\)
\ElsIf{prefetch hit rate \(> 0.60\) and a demand miss is outstanding}
  \State \(S_t\gets\min(S_t+1,S_{\max})\)
\EndIf
\State \(Z\gets\) residency and lookahead leases with depth \(\le S_t\)
\If{this event is a slow boundary}
  \State add fidelity leases to \(Z\)
\EndIf
\If{\(\widehat R(X)>\widehat\epsilon/\kappa\)}
  \State close promotions of minimum replayed cost per unit of \(\widehat R\)
  \State admit them until \(\widehat R(X)\le\widehat\epsilon/\kappa\)
\EndIf
\ForAll{lease \(z\in Z\)}
  \State attach donors \(D\) in increasing counterfactual loss
  \State close \(x=(z,D,\mathcal T)\)
  \If{\(x\) violates \(\widehat R\le\widehat\epsilon/\kappa\), Eq.~\eqref{eq:fit}, or deadlines}
    \State discard \(x\)
  \Else
    \State compute \(G(x\mid X)\) and \(\eta(x\mid X)\) by deadline replay
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\While{some feasible \(x\) has \(G(x\mid X)>0\)}
  \State admit \(x^\star\gets\arg\max_x\eta(x\mid X)\)
  \State apply \(x^\star\) to provisional \(X\) and both accounts
  \State drop conflicting exchanges and replay the rest
\EndWhile
\If{this event is a slow boundary and the candidate would change the published fidelity set and the 100-step fidelity tenure has ended}
  \State reserve the candidate extents inside \(B\) and copy them on the serving GPU
  \If{the reservation would exceed \(B\)}
    \State discard the schedule and leave the published state unchanged
  \Else
    \State score the side probe on provisional \(X\)
    \If{the probe measures \(\Delta\mathrm{PPL}>\epsilon\)}
      \State roll back the last admitted exchange
      \State promote until the probe is within \(\epsilon\) or no promotion fits
      \If{the probe still exceeds \(\epsilon\)}
        \State discard the schedule and leave the published state unchanged
      \Else
        \State submit the admitted schedules \((Z^+,D,\mathcal T)\)
      \EndIf
    \Else
      \State submit the admitted schedules \((Z^+,D,\mathcal T)\)
    \EndIf
  \EndIf
\Else
  \State submit the admitted schedules \((Z^+,D,\mathcal T)\)
\EndIf
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

On a fast event the coordinator submits the surrogate-admitted schedule without a probe. On a slow boundary it submits only when the probe measures \(\Delta\mathrm{PPL}\le\epsilon\). Otherwise it leaves the published state unchanged.

\subsection{Safe Executor}
\label{sec:admit}

The Safe Executor performs step~5 in Figure~\ref{fig:arch}.
Its input is an admitted schedule \((Z^+,D,\mathcal T)\).
Its output is a new published state and telemetry for the next snapshot.
The executor runs the admitted schedule.

\paragraph{Reserve.}
The executor reserves every extent in \(\mathcal T\) in one transaction and checks the transition peak in Eq.~\eqref{eq:fit}.
A destination that must exist before its donor becomes reclaimable is bound to an actual extent during this transaction.
If no compatible extent can be bound, or the peak exceeds \(B\), the executor discards the schedule and leaves the published state unchanged.

\paragraph{Copy and publish.}
\label{sec:publish}
Copies run on the migration stream.
A demotion keeps serving the high representation until the low replacement is published.
An eviction-only donor is reclaimed after its readers finish.
The candidate copies reside in the reserved extents and count toward \(B\). Algorithm~\ref{alg:period} probes those copies on the serving GPU. The executor publishes the new handle only after that probe measures \(\Delta\mathrm{PPL}\le\epsilon\). Until then the forward path uses the old representation.

\paragraph{Reclaim and ready work.}
The executor reclaims an old extent only after its reader count reaches zero and its recorded uses complete.
After routing, groups with published handles issue before groups waiting on a transfer.
A missed lookahead waits only in its assigned group, and routing and top-\(k\) do not change.
A speculative exchange may displace a protected resident only when the replay of its closed exchange has already charged that resident's reload.

\paragraph{Telemetry.}
The executor returns the measurements defined in Section~\ref{sec:monitor}.
They update the next snapshot. If that probe measures \(\Delta\mathrm{PPL}>\epsilon\), Algorithm~\ref{alg:period} rolls back the last admitted exchange. The published handles stay until a later probe is within \(\epsilon\).
