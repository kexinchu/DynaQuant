\section{Design}
\label{sec:design}

\systemname\ has two loops and one admission gate.
The fast loop chooses \(S\) so that the resident set tracks~\eqref{eq:sstar}.
The slow loop chooses which experts occupy high-precision pins, and it changes the pin count only when the fast loop reports slack.
The gate refuses any transition whose bytes are not already reserved.
Figure~\ref{fig:arch} shows the path of one control period.
The predictor that builds \(\hat{U}(S)\) is deliberately local: it is the model's pregate top-\(k\) when the checkpoint exposes pregate logits, and otherwise the empirical selection frequency of that layer over the last \(T_{\mathrm{fast}}\) observations.
A learned corrector is an ablation in Section~\ref{sec:eval}, not part of the claim.
If the predictor overestimates the miss set, \(S^{\star}\) grows until~\eqref{eq:fit} fails and the dense rule takes over.
Over-prediction therefore spends bytes on a wider resident set.
It does not issue an unbounded prefetch.

\subsection{Fast Loop}
\label{sec:fast}

The fast loop runs once per control period of \(T_{\mathrm{fast}}\) MoE layers.
It reads the exponential moving averages of exposed wait and of the miss-set size, updates \(C\) from the bytes and duration of copies that completed on the migration stream, and updates \(T_{\ell}\) from CUDA events around the layer's expert kernels.
It then evaluates~\eqref{eq:overlap} and~\eqref{eq:fit} at the p95 miss size for every integer \(S\) in \([S_{\min},S_{\max}]\).
The range is the number of MoE layers in the model, so the scan is a few dozen predicate checks.
The target is~\eqref{eq:sstar} when the feasible set is non-empty, and \(S_{\min}\) when it is empty.

The applied horizon moves by at most one layer:
\begin{equation}
  S \leftarrow S + \mathrm{clip}(S^{\star}-S,\,-1,\,1),
  \label{eq:step}
\end{equation}
except inside a hysteresis band.
If the predicted change in exposed wait from taking the step is smaller than \(\theta_{h}\) seconds, \(S\) stays put.
Exposed wait for a candidate is \(\max(0,\, M^{\mathrm{p95}}_{\mathrm{miss}}(S)/C - S \cdot T_{\ell})\).
The band is what keeps a noisy p95 estimate from flipping \(S\) every period.
Because each step has size at most one, a constant target is reached in at most \(S_{\max}-S_{\min}\) periods, and a target that jumps across the range cannot make the cache absorb that jump in one period.
This is the same bound used to keep a horizon controller from oscillating, with the target now supplied by~\eqref{eq:sstar} instead of by a weighted difference of wait and eviction counts.

When the feasible set is empty the loop additionally marks the period dense.
Prefetch admission then accepts experts from the current layer's predicted set first, and it does not extend the speculative set past \(S_{\min}\).
When the feasible set is non-empty the loop marks the period sparse and computes the slack
\begin{equation}
  B_{\mathrm{slack}}
  \;=\;
  B - \mathrm{bytes}\!\left(R \cup \hat{U}(S)\right)
  \label{eq:slack}
\end{equation}
after the horizon step has been applied.
\(B_{\mathrm{slack}}\) is the only byte quantity the slow loop is allowed to turn into new high-precision pins.

\subsection{Slow Loop}
\label{sec:slow}

The slow loop runs every \(T_{\mathrm{slow}}\) forward steps and is the only writer of high-precision identities.
For each layer it maintains a score \(s_{\ell,e}\).
After a step with \(N_{\ell}\) routed positions, the observation for expert \(e\) is the mean selected router mass
\begin{equation}
  c_{\ell,e}
  \;=\;
  \frac{1}{N_{\ell}}
  \sum_{t=1}^{N_{\ell}}
  \mathbf{1}[e \in \mathcal{R}_{\ell,t}]\; p_{\ell,t,e},
  \label{eq:hotness}
\end{equation}
and \(c_{\ell,e}=0\) when \(e\) is not selected, so unused experts decay.
The score is \(s_{\ell,e} \leftarrow \alpha s_{\ell,e} + (1-\alpha) c_{\ell,e}\).
No task label and no online quality signal enter \(s_{\ell,e}\).

In Phase~II the pin count is the constant from~\eqref{eq:phase2-nhi}.
In Phase~III the pin count starts at zero.
On a sparse period the loop may increment \(n_{\mathrm{hi},\ell}\) by one if \(B_{\mathrm{slack}} \ge m^{\mathrm{hi}}_{\ell}-m^{\mathrm{lo}}_{\ell}\) when a low-precision copy is resident, or \(B_{\mathrm{slack}} \ge m^{\mathrm{hi}}_{\ell}\) when it is not.
On a dense period the loop increments nothing.
If the fast loop reports that the current pins have made~\eqref{eq:fit} infeasible at every \(S\), the slow loop decrements \(n_{\mathrm{hi},\ell}\) by one.
At most one increment or one decrement occurs per layer per slow period.
That rate limit is what prevents a single prefill from demoting the decode pins and a single decode step from promoting a prefill-only expert.

Identities follow the count.
An outsider replaces the lowest-scoring incumbent only when its score exceeds the incumbent by a margin \(\delta\).
The margin is the mechanism that absorbs the near-ties visible when many experts have similar mass.
A replacement is one demotion and one promotion, submitted as a pair, demotion first.
Phase~I performs neither step.

The separation of timescales is an assumption we measure rather than a parameter we hide.
\(T_{\mathrm{slow}}\) is set to a multiple of \(T_{\mathrm{fast}}\) large enough that a pin change is constant across several horizon updates.
Section~\ref{sec:eval} reports the waiting-time variance in the periods immediately after a pin change.
If that variance dominates the variance from \(S\) itself, the lexicographic split is the wrong coupling and the paper's controller is the wrong design.

\subsection{Admission and Pools}
\label{sec:admit}

Device memory for expert weights is divided into per-layer pools of fixed-size blocks, one block size per precision.
A high-precision block cannot be allocated from the low-precision pool.
Fixed blocks remove variable-size external fragmentation from the expert allocator; the free path is a free list, not the general-purpose device allocator.
A separate staging pool of \(K\) blocks, sized to the larger representation, covers the interval in which the new block is filled and the old block is still published.

Every promotion, demotion, and prefetch calls \texttt{try\_reserve} before it acquires a block or enqueues a copy.
The reservation checks three counters: the destination tier's capacity, the staging capacity, and the sum of published plus reserved expert bytes against \(B\).
A failed reservation leaves the published set unchanged.
The caller retries on a later period from that published set, not from the set it wished to publish.
Because reservations are subtracted from \(B_{\mathrm{slack}}\) and from the fast loop's byte account before the copy starts, the pin loop and the prefetch loop cannot both spend the same byte.

Equation~\eqref{eq:fit} is therefore an online invariant of \systemname-owned memory: resident blocks, staging blocks, and the conversion workspace reserved at startup.
It is not an invariant of the process.
A KV-cache growth or a framework allocation beyond its declared reserve can still exhaust the device.
Startup compares the sum of declared reserves against available memory and refuses to enter the loop if the sum exceeds the device.
The implementation does not interpose on a later allocator call.
The evaluation reports both the runtime's counters and the process peak so that a reserve violation is visible as a process-peak failure rather than as a counter failure.

\subsection{Publication}
\label{sec:publish}

A published handle is a precision tag, a device pointer, and the quantization metadata for one expert.
The forward path resolves the router’s expert identifiers to handles, takes a read lease, and records a per-stream last-use event after the expert kernel is launched.
It never writes a handle in place.

A transition fills a destination block on the migration stream.
The worker publishes the new handle only after that stream’s completion event.
The old handle is reclaimed only after its lease count is zero and every recorded last-use event has completed.
The two waits cover the interval in which the forward path has read the old pointer and has not yet launched the kernel that uses it.
Demotion publishes the low-precision handle first and then reclaims the high-precision block under the same rule.
The compute stream does not synchronize with the migration stream.
A dispatch that arrives during the copy observes the old handle; the next dispatch observes the new one.

Tokens in a layer are issued in residency order.
Tokens whose experts are already published run first.
Tokens whose experts are in flight wait for that copy and run as the copy completes, concurrently with the resident tokens of the same layer.
This overlap is the reason a single miss is charged as at most one transfer on the migration stream rather than as a stall in front of every expert kernel.
The evaluation measures the exposed remainder of that transfer.
The design bound is one transfer of size \(m_{\ell,e}\); the bound is false if a miss triggers an eviction that reloads an expert the same step still needs, which is the failure mode~\eqref{eq:fit} is there to forbid.

\subsection{Control Period}
\label{sec:alg}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:period} is one period of the two loops.
Phase classification uses the measured pool sizes and does not change unless \(B\) is reconfigured.
The hotness update is omitted from the listing; it is~\eqref{eq:hotness} and runs on every forward step, while lines that change \(n_{\mathrm{hi}}\) run only on slow periods.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{One \systemname\ control period.}
\label{alg:period}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require usable budget $B$, bounds $[S_{\min},S_{\max}]$, hysteresis $\theta_{h}$, current horizon $S$
\Require resident set $R$, pin counts $n_{\mathrm{hi},\ell}$, measured $C$, $T_{\ell}$, p95 miss bytes
\If{$B \ge P_{\mathrm{hi}}$}
  \State load the all-high assignment and \Return \Comment{Phase I}
\EndIf
\If{$P_{\mathrm{lo}} \le B$ and $B < P_{\mathrm{hi}}$}
  \State $S \gets 0$ \Comment{Phase II, Prediction P1}
  \State set $n_{\mathrm{hi}}$ from the proportional form of Eq.~\eqref{eq:phase2-nhi}
  \State replace at most one pin per layer if the hotness margin $\delta$ holds
  \State \Return
\EndIf
\State $F \gets \{ s \in [S_{\min},S_{\max}] : \text{overlap and fit hold at p95 miss bytes} \}$
\If{$F = \emptyset$}
  \State $S^{\star} \gets S_{\min}$; dense $\gets$ true \Comment{Prediction P3, dense}
\Else
  \State $S^{\star} \gets \min F$; dense $\gets$ false
\EndIf
\State step $S$ toward $S^{\star}$ by at most one layer, unless the predicted wait change is below $\theta_{h}$
\If{dense}
  \State admit prefetches from the current layer only; do not increment any $n_{\mathrm{hi},\ell}$
  \If{pins make every horizon infeasible}
    \State decrement one $n_{\mathrm{hi},\ell}$
  \EndIf
\Else
  \State compute slack from Eq.~\eqref{eq:slack}
  \If{slack covers one promotion}
    \State increment one $n_{\mathrm{hi},\ell}$ and pin the highest-scoring unpinned expert
  \EndIf
\EndIf
\State submit admitted copies on the migration stream; publish on completion
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{What Is Intentionally Not Adapted}
\label{sec:non-goals}

Top-\(k\), expert architecture, and the router's parameters stay at the checkpoint values.
\systemname\ does not skip an expert to save a miss, because a skip changes the function being served.
The low- and high-precision tensors are produced offline by the same quantization recipe for every expert; the online decision is which tensor is published, not a new set of scales.
There is no per-request retuning of \(\alpha\), \(\delta\), \(\theta_{h}\), \(T_{\mathrm{fast}}\), or \(T_{\mathrm{slow}}\).
Those constants are chosen on a calibration split and frozen.
Section~\ref{sec:eval} states the split.
A deployment that needs a different \(B\) changes \(\PhiRatio\) and therefore the phase, without a new calibration of the horizon.
