\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) layer routes each token to a few expert subnetworks, so parameter count grows without a proportional increase in per-token arithmetic~\cite{shazeer2017outrageously,fedus2022switch,lepikhin2021gshard}.
Deployed models such as Mixtral, DeepSeek-V2, and Qwen3 keep tens to hundreds of billions of parameters resident in the expert pool while activating only a few billion per token~\cite{jiang2024mixtral,deepseekv2,qwen3technicalreport}.
On one accelerator the expert pool dominates the footprint: in the models we study it is about 95--96\% of the weights.
Device memory must also hold non-expert parameters, activations, the KV cache, and runtime buffers.
The resulting constraint is a byte budget \(B\) for expert weights.
Every expert is in one of three states under that budget: absent from the device, resident at low precision, or resident at high precision.
The same bytes also determine how many future layers of experts can be prefetched before the cache overflows.

Two lines of work each freeze one of those choices.
Precision-oriented systems compress experts with post-training quantization and, in the adaptive case, spend a scarce high-precision quota on experts that the router uses heavily~\cite{lin2024awq,frantar2022gptq,duanmu2025mxmoe,tang2024hobbit}.
Their operating assumption is that a low-precision copy of every routed expert stays addressable on the device, so a cache miss never occurs.
Prefetch-oriented systems treat device memory as a cache and overlap expert transfers with compute by fetching the experts of the next \(S\) layers~\cite{song2024promoe,eliseev2023fast,xue2024moe,shen2025expertflow}.
Their operating assumption is that expert byte size is fixed, so the only decision is which experts to stage and how large \(S\) should be.
Each assumption defines a real deployment, and each is the wrong deployment for the other budget.

The optimal expert prefetch horizon is set by precision, through one ratio.
Let \(P_{\mathrm{lo}}\) be the number of bytes required to store every routed expert at low precision, and \(P_{\mathrm{hi}}\) the same quantity at high precision.
\[
  \PhiRatio \;=\; B / P_{\mathrm{lo}}.
\]
Section~\ref{sec:model} derives three regimes from \(\PhiRatio\) and from the overlap identity that a horizon of \(S\) layers hides at most \(S \cdot T_{\ell} \cdot C\) bytes of transfer, where \(T_{\ell}\) is the per-layer compute time and \(C\) is the effective host-to-device bandwidth.
When \(\PhiRatio \ge P_{\mathrm{hi}}/P_{\mathrm{lo}}\), the pool fits at high precision and neither actuator is needed.
When \(1 \le \PhiRatio < P_{\mathrm{hi}}/P_{\mathrm{lo}}\), the pool fits at low precision and does not fit at high precision.
The latency-minimizing horizon is then \(S^{\star}=0\): every expert can stay resident, and leftover bytes, \(B-P_{\mathrm{lo}}\), buy high-precision pins.
When \(\PhiRatio < 1\), some experts are absent.
\(S^{\star}\) is the smallest horizon that hides the predicted miss set and whose speculative bytes still fit in \(B\); high-precision pins are taken only from the slack above that horizon.
A second, faster variable sits inside the third regime.
Prefill and large batches touch a large fraction of the experts in a layer, while the next decode step touches far fewer (Section~\ref{sec:prelim}).
The feasible set for \(S\) can be empty during prefill and non-empty during decode at the same \(\PhiRatio\).
The active actuator therefore changes inside one request.

\systemname\ is a runtime that tracks this model.
At startup it measures packed expert sizes and classifies \(\PhiRatio\).
A slow loop maintains high-precision identities with an exponentially smoothed hotness score and a hysteresis margin, and it changes the pin count by at most one expert per layer per period.
A fast loop moves \(S\) by at most one layer per period toward the model's \(S^{\star}\), using the precision at which the expert will actually be transferred.
Admission control reserves bytes before any promotion, demotion, or prefetch, and a versioned handle becomes visible to the forward pass only after its copy completes.
Routing semantics are unchanged: the runtime does not drop experts and does not alter top-\(k\).

This paper makes four contributions.
\begin{itemize}
  \item We formulate expert-weight memory as one byte budget with three residency states, and we show that the ratio \(\PhiRatio\) plus the overlap identity predict which actuator---high-precision pins, a low-precision resident floor, or a prefetch horizon---can reduce exposed transfer latency.
  \item We give a two-timescale controller that tracks the predicted horizon with a bounded update and spends residual bytes on high-precision pins only when the latency band remains feasible.
  \item We specify the memory invariant and the publication protocol required for precision migration and prefetch to share that budget without a forward-path dependence on an in-flight copy.
  \item We specify a measurement protocol that sweeps \(\PhiRatio\) on one stack, tests the model's predictions before any system comparison, and treats precision-only residency, horizon-only prefetch, and their naive composition as the baselines that occupy the boundary policies.
\end{itemize}
