\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) layer routes each token to a small subset of experts, so capacity can grow without a proportional increase in per-token computation~\cite{shazeer2017outrageously,fedus2022switch,lepikhin2021gshard}.
The expert weights still have to be stored or fetched.
Models from Mixtral and Qwen3 to DeepSeek-V2 and GLaM keep tens to hundreds of billions of parameters, and in the largest case a trillion, while activating only a fraction of them per token~\cite{jiang2024mixtral,qwen3technicalreport,deepseekv2,du2022glam}.
On one GPU those weights compete with non-expert parameters, the KV cache, activations, and runtime workspaces.
When the experts cannot all remain at the desired precision, the system has to compress them and move them across the host-to-device link.

How should one GPU spend its expert bytes and its host-to-device link so that Mixture-of-Experts inference lowers tail latency, keeps measured \(\Delta\mathrm{PPL}\) within \(\epsilon\), and never holds more expert bytes than the budget?

Quantization fixes each expert's precision before serving~\cite{frantar2022gptq,lin2024awq}.
Mixed-precision methods choose that precision under a memory or accuracy target~\cite{duanmu2025mxmoe}, and expert caches and prefetchers then decide which experts stay on the GPU and when a missing expert is copied, usually for one fixed precision~\cite{eliseev2023fast,song2024promoe,xue2024moe}.
HOBBIT and DyMoE already run the low-precision copy, the cache, and the prefetch in one system~\cite{tang2024hobbit,huang2026dymoe}.

Four measurements show why a fixed split of those bytes fails.
Perplexity rises as more experts move to the smaller, low-precision copy, so keeping an expert at high precision both spends bytes and makes a later transfer larger.
WikiText, GSM8K, and HumanEval at the same layer call different experts, so a precision map or a resident set taken from one task misses the next.
In prefill the bytes that can overlap compute grow with the batch while the selected bytes stay nearly flat; in decode the selected bytes grow and the overlap window does not.
Raising the high-precision share from 25\% to 40\% also changes how many experts can stay on the GPU and how many layers ahead a copy can be staged.
Section~\ref{sec:bg} reports these measurements.
Section~\ref{sec:design} spends the same bytes by demoting or evicting experts already on the GPU and using the freed bytes for the copy it is about to admit.

We make three contributions.
\begin{itemize}
  \item We measure one expert-memory budget and one host-to-device queue with three spends: a larger high-precision copy, keeping an expert on the GPU, and copying an expert before it is used (Section~\ref{sec:bg}). A fixed split cannot compare these spends when the routed experts and the prefill--decode mix change, and systems that already quantize, cache, and prefetch still decide the three spends separately.
  \item We design \systemname\ to demote or evict experts already on the GPU to free bytes for the copy it admits, as specified in Section~\ref{sec:design}. The executor holds every transition's expert-memory peak within \(B\). Admission requires \(\widehat R\le\widehat\epsilon/\kappa\), with \(\kappa>1\) fixed on the calibration prompts. A state is published only when a side probe measures \(\Delta\mathrm{PPL}\le\epsilon\).
  \item We evaluate \systemname\ on one GPU with Qwen3-30B, Qwen3-Next-80B, and Phi-3.5-MoE. Latency replays a fixed order of MMLU-Pro, GPQA, AIME25, GSM8K, and HumanEval, split by input/output length, at batch size 8. The feasibility cap is \(\Delta\mathrm{PPL}\) on the mixed evaluation prompts. Downstream scores use the same five tasks, weighted by item count. \systemname's \(\Delta\mathrm{PPL}\) on those prompts is 0.62, 0.52, and 0.82. These three values are within \(\epsilon=0.70\), \(0.60\), and \(0.90\). Its averages on the five tasks are 73.49, 79.04, and 60.73 against the all-high averages 74.37, 79.55, and 61.36, lower by 0.88, 0.51, and 0.63 points (1.18\%, 0.64\%, and 1.03\%) (Table~\ref{tab:eval-accuracy}). Under the same expert-memory budget, p95 TPOT is 11.6\% lower than HOBBIT on Qwen3-30B, 23\% lower on Qwen3-Next-80B, and 27.1\% lower on Phi-3.5-MoE (Table~\ref{tab:eval-main}). The lowest p95 centers in Table~\ref{tab:eval-main} are 43.7\,ms, 117\,ms, and 51.8\,ms. The rows at those centers have \(\Delta\mathrm{PPL}\) above \(\epsilon=0.70\), \(0.60\), and \(0.90\). Under Poisson arrivals from a client on the same host, SLO goodput is 0.82 requests/s against HOBBIT's 0.69 on Qwen3-30B (Table~\ref{tab:eval-serving}).
\end{itemize}
