\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) layer routes each token to a small subset of experts, so capacity can grow without a proportional increase in per-token computation~\cite{shazeer2017outrageously,fedus2022switch,lepikhin2021gshard}.
The expert weights still have to be stored or fetched.
Models from Mixtral and Qwen3 to DeepSeek-V2 and GLaM keep tens to hundreds of billions of parameters, and in the largest case a trillion, while activating only a fraction of them per token~\cite{jiang2024mixtral,qwen3technicalreport,deepseekv2,du2022glam}.
On one GPU those weights compete with non-expert parameters, the KV cache, activations, and runtime workspaces.
When the experts cannot all remain at the desired precision, the system has to compress them and move them across the host-to-device link.

How should a limited GPU allocate its expert memory and host-to-device transfer bandwidth among fidelity, residency, and lookahead, so that Mixture-of-Experts inference reduces tail latency, keeps measured quality within \(\epsilon\), and holds the expert-memory peak within budget?
These two budgets meet in three uses, defined in Section~\ref{sec:serving}.
A byte assigned to fidelity cannot also retain another expert or stage a future one, and host-to-device bandwidth decides whether that early transfer finishes before its deadline.

Prior work has effective policies for parts of this problem.
Quantization and mixed-precision methods choose expert representations under a memory or accuracy target~\cite{lin2024awq,frantar2022gptq,duanmu2025mxmoe}.
Expert caches and prefetchers decide which experts to keep and when to transfer missing experts, commonly under a fixed representation size~\cite{song2024promoe,eliseev2023fast,xue2024moe,shen2025expertflow}.
Mixed-precision offloading shows that lower-precision transfers, caching, and prefetching can run in one system~\cite{tang2024hobbit,huang2026dymoe}.

Section~\ref{sec:bg} measures four facts that a fixed policy does not absorb.
Perplexity rises as more experts move to the lower tier, and the higher tier is the larger copy.
WikiText, GSM8K, and HumanEval dispatch different experts at the same layer.
Prefill and decode push the traffic that lookahead cannot hide in opposite directions.
Raising the high-precision share changes both how many experts can stay resident and which staging depth hides their transfers.

These measurements are one allocation.
Fidelity, residency, and lookahead spend the same expert-memory bytes and the same host-to-device queue.
Section~\ref{sec:design} specifies the controller that allocates them.

We make three contributions.
\begin{itemize}
  \item We observe that expert memory and host-to-device bandwidth form one budget with three uses. A byte spent on fidelity cannot retain another expert or stage a future one, and a transfer that lookahead starts still has to finish before it is used (Section~\ref{sec:bg}). A fixed partition or a fixed priority cannot compare these costs as the routed experts and the prefill--decode mix change, and systems that already combine quantization, caching, and prefetching still admit the three uses separately.
  \item We design \systemname, which allocates this budget by admitting one exchange at a time. The executor holds every transition's expert-memory peak within a hard budget \(B\). The exchange names the lease it requests and the leases released to fund it, and the controller admits it only when that release lowers predicted exposed wait and \(\widehat R\le\widehat\epsilon\). That quality test estimates \(\Delta Q\le\epsilon\); it is not a measured-quality guarantee. Fidelity, residency, and lookahead share that path and one byte account, and a representation is published only after its copy completes.
  \item We evaluate \systemname\ on one GPU with Qwen3-30B, Qwen3-Next-80B, and Phi-3.5-MoE. Latency replays a fixed order of MMLU-Pro, GPQA, AIME25, GSM8K, and HumanEval, split by input/output length, at batch size 8. Quality uses WikiText-2 together with the same five tasks, weighted by sample size (200, 198, 30, 200, and 164). \systemname's score is 1.18\%, 0.64\%, and 1.03\% below the all-high reference on Qwen3-30B, Qwen3-Next-80B, and Phi-3.5-MoE (Table~\ref{tab:eval-accuracy}). Under the same expert-memory budget, p95 TPOT is 11.6\% lower than HOBBIT on Qwen3-30B, 23\% lower on Qwen3-Next-80B, and 27.1\% lower on Phi-3.5-MoE (Table~\ref{tab:eval-main}).
\end{itemize}
