\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

Five questions follow: tail latency at fixed expert-memory budgets (RQ1), a shared online assignment against a fixed order and a frozen one (RQ2), which of the three removals in Figure~\ref{fig:eval-mechanisms} moves the Table~\ref{tab:eval-main} gap (RQ3), whether the online surrogates and the controller are cheap enough to run (RQ4), and how offered load changes end-to-end latency and SLO goodput (RQ5).

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:eval-setup}

\paragraph{Platform and models.}
We evaluate on a single NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPU with 48\,GB of device memory. Table~\ref{tab:eval-models} summarizes each checkpoint's routing structure and the two packed tiers of routed experts. For Qwen3-Next, the shared expert is not routed and remains resident with the non-expert weights.

For Qwen3-30B, the expert arena occupies 29.74\,GB, corresponding to \(\PhiRatio=1.931\). The low- and high-tier expert packs occupy 15.40\,GB and 57.98\,GB, respectively, giving \(\rho=3.76\); keeping both packs resident would require 73.38\,GB and therefore exceeds device capacity. Host-to-device bandwidth is 24.86\,GB/s.

Table~\ref{tab:eval-main} and the later latency figures for this model use \(B=20.0\)\,GB (\(\PhiRatio=1.3\)), 9.74\,GB below the 29.74\,GB arena. The \(\Phi=0.7\) run in Section~\ref{sec:eval-end-to-end} uses \(B=10.78\)\,GB.

\paragraph{Workloads and quality.}
The prompts are drawn from a mixed workload comprising MMLU-Pro~\cite{wang2024mmlu}, GPQA~\cite{rein2024gpqa}, AIME25~\cite{zhang2025aime25}, GSM8K~\cite{cobbe2021gsm8k}, and HumanEval~\cite{chen2021evaluating}. We construct workload slices according to the input/output token-length ratio: prefill-heavy requests have the largest input-to-output ratios, decode-heavy requests have the largest output-to-input ratios, and balanced requests minimize the difference between input and output lengths. Unless otherwise specified, inference uses a batch size of 8. We measure model-path time to first token (TTFT) and time per output token (TPOT) on a fixed replay order. Section~\ref{sec:eval-serving} offers the same prompts from an open-loop client and includes queue wait, serialization, loopback transport, tokenization, offered load, and goodput.

Calibration prompts determine the sensitivity estimates, predictor width, controller constants, \(\kappa\), and baseline configurations. Evaluation prompts are disjoint from the calibration prompts and from the 4 probe sequences, and no parameter is refit on the test set. \textsc{Static-Joint} keeps the assignment frozen from the calibration mix. \systemname\ may change the assignment on the evaluation stream.

All-high is the quality reference: every routed expert uses the high tier, and a missing expert is fetched on the residency path used by the other policies.

The scores in Table~\ref{tab:eval-accuracy} use 200 MMLU-Pro items, 198 GPQA items, 30 AIME25 items, 200 GSM8K items, and 164 HumanEval items. Avg.\ weights the five accuracies by these counts.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
Each baseline fixes one of these decisions. \emph{Uniform-low} places every routed expert at the low tier. \emph{Static-mixed} keeps that low-tier pack resident and spends \(B-P_{\mathrm{lo}}\) on fidelity increments for the largest-\(q_i\) experts that fit, then freezes the assignment. At the Table~\ref{tab:eval-main} budgets \(\Phi\ge 1\), lookahead is off, so no expert is transferred on the measured requests. \emph{Residency-only} leaves \(B-P_{\mathrm{lo}}\) idle and \emph{Lookahead-only} stages no expert; both are the Uniform-low point. At \(B=10.78\)\,GB the low-tier pack does not fit, so Static-mixed adds no fidelity increment and absent experts transfer at low precision. \textsc{Seq-FR} and \textsc{Seq-RF} test whether independently optimizing the same decisions in a fixed order is sufficient: \textsc{Seq-FR} assigns fidelity before residency and lookahead, whereas \textsc{Seq-RF} assigns residency before fidelity and lookahead. \textsc{Static-Joint} assigns \(B\) among the three uses on calibration statistics and keeps the feasible assignment with the lowest latency. It then freezes that assignment on the evaluation stream. \emph{Fixed-partition} instead reserves independent quotas for the three uses, testing the value of allowing memory to move across them.

For controlled comparisons, every \systemname\ policy receives the same expert-memory budget \(B\), expert representations, kernels, request stream, and transition interface. Baselines that require calibration receive the same calibration traces and search budget as \systemname. HOBBIT~\cite{tang2024hobbit} and DyMoE~\cite{huang2026dymoe} have no public implementation. We reimplemented both from the papers under the same expert-memory budget, packed tiers, kernels, and request stream. HOBBIT uses its dynamic loader, adaptive prefetcher, and multi-level cache. In this comparison a cache miss on a less critical expert loads the low-precision copy. DyMoE uses its importance estimator, depth-aware schedule, cache, and lookahead prefetcher. That schedule assigns the high and low tiers already packed for the comparison, FP16/INT4 on Qwen3-30B and Phi-3.5-MoE and INT4/INT2 on Qwen3-Next-80B, and every expert the router selected is executed. DyMoE's published 4/0 schedule omits routed experts. Executing every expert the router selected keeps Table~\ref{tab:eval-main}'s \(\Delta\mathrm{PPL}\) on the same top-\(k\) set as the other rows. The 4/0 schedule is not one of those rows. HOBBIT's other measured points at these budgets are its rows in Table~\ref{tab:eval-knobs}. On the same kernels and request stream, that 4/0 schedule has \(\Delta\mathrm{PPL}\) 0.81, 0.71, and 0.96, above \(\epsilon=0.70\), \(0.60\), and \(0.90\). Its p95 centers are 49.4\,ms [48.0, 50.8], 170\,ms [161, 179], and 69.5\,ms [65.7, 73.3]. Each bracket overlaps the DyMoE bracket in Table~\ref{tab:eval-main}.

\paragraph{Metrics and statistics.}
We report time to first token (TTFT), time per output token (TPOT), and exposed expert-transfer wait on the 792 requests counted above. Each run uses five warm-up iterations and then those 792 requests on each of five seeds. Policies that share a seed see the same request order. For each policy and seed, the request-level p95 TPOT is computed on that seed's 792 requests. We report the median of those five p95 values, and the bracket is their minimum and maximum. TTFT is the request-level p95 on the same requests. Comparisons use these centers. Where one bracket lies entirely below another, every paired seed difference has that sign, and the difference is at least the gap between the brackets.

\paragraph{Memory accounting and admission.}
A run is valid only if arena occupancy remains within the expert-memory budget \(B\) and its exchange log can reconstruct the reported allocation. NVML samples process-level GPU memory every 2\,ms, and Table~\ref{tab:eval-main} reports that high-water mark as Peak.

\subsection{Shared Online Allocation and Simpler Policies}
\label{sec:eval-necessity}

\paragraph{RQ2: When does a shared online allocation improve on a simpler policy?}
\textsc{Seq-FR} commits bytes to fidelity first, and \textsc{Seq-RF} commits them to residency first. On the stationary balanced-length slice neither assignment can move those bytes to the other use (Figure~\ref{fig:eval-necessity}(a)).

After the slice becomes decode-heavy, lookahead's share rises and fidelity's falls (Figure~\ref{fig:eval-necessity}(b)). Decode leaves less time before use. On this Qwen3-30B slice, two fidelity publications finish before that measured interval. Each probe is the 16-step probe in Section~\ref{sec:impl}, \(16\times 45.8=732.8\,\mathrm{ms}\), so the two probes take \(1465.6\,\mathrm{ms}\). Each publication then copies one expert, \((57.98-15.40)/128=0.3327\,\mathrm{GB}\) at \(24.86\,\mathrm{GB/s}\), which is \(13.4\,\mathrm{ms}\); the two copies take \(26.8\,\mathrm{ms}\). Both probes are within \(\epsilon\), so the rollback time is \(0\,\mathrm{ms}\). A request that overlaps one publication waits \(732.8+13.4=746.2\,\mathrm{ms}\) for the serving GPU. Table~\ref{tab:eval-main}'s p95 remains the service time of its timed requests, and the p95 in Figure~\ref{fig:eval-necessity}(b) remains the service time of its measured interval.

\subsection{Mechanism Attribution}
\label{sec:eval-mechanism}

\paragraph{RQ3: Which of these removals moves the Table~\ref{tab:eval-main} gap?}
We isolate the contribution of \systemname's key mechanisms by removing one at a time while holding the workload, expert-memory budget \(B\), and execution path fixed within each experiment. \emph{Independent-value} keeps the shared arena, the \(\widehat R\) cap, and the executor. It scores the requested lease and its donor as separate transfer times, \(p_{\mathrm{req}}m/C-p_{\mathrm{donor}}m/C\), and does not replay the shared queue. \emph{Slack-only} restricts admission to currently available slack and disables donor selection. \emph{Fixed-partition}, defined in Section~\ref{sec:eval-setup}, replaces the shared arena with separate quotas for fidelity, residency, and lookahead.

\subsection{Offered Load}
\label{sec:eval-serving}

\paragraph{RQ5: End-to-end latency and goodput.}
On the same GPU and the same expert-memory budgets, a client on this host offers the mixed prompts under Poisson arrivals and under a two-state process that alternates a slower and a faster Poisson rate at the same mean. End-to-end time starts at the client send and includes serialization, loopback transport, tokenization, waiting until the GPU takes the request, and the model path. The GPU runs at most eight requests at once. A completion counts toward goodput when its end-to-end TTFT is at most twice Uniform-low's p95 TTFT in Table~\ref{tab:eval-main}: 5.2\,s, 13.6\,s, and 3.6\,s. In Table~\ref{tab:eval-serving}, \systemname's end-to-end bracket meets that objective and HOBBIT's does not. The bracket is the minimum and maximum over five seeds.
