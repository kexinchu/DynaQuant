\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What Would Falsify the Account}
\label{sec:falsify}

The account is a latency model plus a proxy that spends slack.
They fail separately.
If E2 finds that the minimizing horizon is insensitive to prefetch size, then~\eqref{eq:overlap} is missing a term that dominates \(m\), and precision does not belong in the horizon calculation.
The likely missing term is a kernel-launch or dequantization cost that scales with the number of experts rather than with bytes.
In that world the right design is the precision-only policy whenever the floor fits, and a horizon policy whose \(S\) is chosen from \(T_{\ell}\) alone whenever it does not.
\systemname’s coupling would be an unnecessary constraint.

If E2 finds that idle \(C\) and in-overlap \(C\) produce horizon errors of several layers, the scalar overlap identity is the wrong plant model even if a closed-loop controller can still hunt for a good \(S\).
The paper would then be a control paper about tracking a noisy wait signal, which is a weaker and different claim than P2.
We would rather report that negative than retune \(\theta_{h}\) until the controller looks accurate.

If E5 finds that hotness-ranked pins do not separate from static mixed precision inside the latency band, the slow loop’s identity updates are not earning their migration traffic.
The quota rule in~\eqref{eq:phase2-nhi} and the slack rule in~\eqref{eq:slack} could still be right.
The system we would recommend is then a static ranking inside a controller-chosen quota, and the task-disjoint top-10 result in Section~\ref{sec:prelim} would be a statement about calibration shift that our online score failed to track.
That outcome is reportable from the same tables.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

The implementation is one GPU.
An expert-parallel deployment splits \(E_{\ell}\) across devices, and \(\PhiRatio\) becomes a per-device quantity that also depends on the router’s balance.
Nothing in the model forbids that reading, and nothing in the prototype measures it.

Host memory holds both packed tiers for the life of the process.
The device budget is the object of the invariant; the host duplicate is a cost paid at load time.
A deployment that cannot store both tiers must quantize on the promotion path, which adds a term to transition latency that Algorithm~\ref{alg:period} does not hide.

The lexicographic rule prefers a feasible horizon over a pin.
Under a very tight \(B\) the slack is zero for the entire request and \systemname\ reduces to the horizon-only policy.
That reduction is the correct output of P4, and it means the system has no quality advantage over horizon-only in the tightest Phase~III budgets.
We state the quality claim only inside the band defined in E5.

Shared experts that the checkpoint always executes are charged to the non-expert reserve, not to \(B\).
Qwen3-Next has one such expert per layer.
Treating it as a routed expert would inflate \(P_{\mathrm{lo}}\) and shift \(\PhiRatio\).

The p95 ring is a short history.
A workload shift shorter than the ring, or a slow loop that has not yet decayed the previous task’s scores, mis-states both \(S^{\star}\) and the pin identities.
E3’s text-to-code boundary, using the three workloads whose top-10 sets were disjoint, is the measurement of that lag.
We do not claim a bound on lag in periods; we report it.

Cache-aware issue order assumes that tokens in a layer may execute in any order relative to each other.
That is true for the standard grouped-GEMM dispatch and false if a future kernel fused the experts in identifier order.
The ablation that disables reordering measures the exposed-wait gap on today’s kernels.

\subsection{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

The preliminary numbers in Section~\ref{sec:prelim} and the results of E0 are different samples until E0 is run.
We will replace Table~\ref{tab:density} with the E0 table if the two disagree, rather than explaining the disagreement in a caption.

ShareGPT buckets under-represent very long contexts.
A 128k-token prefill changes both the unique-expert ratio and the KV reserve, and the KV reserve eats into the device before \(B\) does.
Our budgets assume the declared KV reserve is honored.
They do not describe a serving stack that over-admits context length.

Quality subsets are a few hundred items.
Wilson intervals on those subsets are wide for small effects.
H5 is a claim about separation of intervals, so a small true effect on this item set will correctly fail to reject the null.
We will not respond to that failure by switching to a larger, post-hoc item set.

Ascend and NVIDIA stacks do not share a kernel implementation.
A \(C\) comparison across them confounds bandwidth with software.
H2’s device clause is therefore stated as a change in the measured in-overlap \(C\), and we report that \(C\), rather than as a pure hardware effect.

\subsection{Reproducibility and Ethics}
\label{sec:ethics}

The latency corpus is a public conversational dataset.
Quality benchmarks are public exam-style and code datasets.
The study does not recruit participants, does not collect new user data, and does not transmit prompts to a third-party API.
Artifacts will contain prompt identifiers and scores, not a redistribution of any dataset whose license forbids it.

The release accompanying an accepted paper is the controller, the pool runtime, the trace utilities that compute the unique-expert ratio and \(\PhiRatio\), and one configuration file per reported cell.
Random seeds, checkpoint revisions, and the calibration-test split identifier are part of each artifact.
We will not release a binary that omits the reservation counters.
A result that cannot be checked against \(B\) is not a result under this protocol.
