\section{Background and Motivation}
\label{sec:bg}

\subsection{Expert Memory on One Accelerator}
\label{sec:serving}

An MoE layer routes each token to \(k_{\ell}\) experts.
The router determines which experts execute.
Expert-choice routing lets each expert select its tokens~\cite{zhou2022expertchoice}.
It does not determine where their weights reside or when they arrive on the device.
A selected expert that is absent must be transferred from host memory, and a demand transfer exposes that time on the forward path.

Memory for non-expert parameters, the KV cache, activations, and fixed workspaces is reserved first.
The remaining capacity is the expert-memory budget \(B\).
Host memory stores a packed low-precision representation and a packed high-precision representation of every routed expert, with aggregate footprints \(P_{\mathrm{lo}}\) and \(P_{\mathrm{hi}}\).
We write \(\PhiRatio=B/P_{\mathrm{lo}}\) and \(\rho=P_{\mathrm{hi}}/P_{\mathrm{lo}}\).
At any instant an expert is absent or resident at low or high precision.
The forward pass can use only a published resident representation.

The host-to-device bandwidth budget is the measured copy rate \(C\) of the migration queue.
\(C\) is the rate of that one queue.
Representation size, whether the expert stays resident, and when its transfer occupies the queue decide which transfers consume this rate.

The same expert-memory bytes have three uses.
The extra bytes of a higher-precision representation are \emph{fidelity}.
\emph{Residency} keeps an expert on the device so that a later access requires no transfer.
\emph{Lookahead} stages an absent expert early enough for its transfer to overlap preceding computation.
Lookahead is a timed transition from absence to residency.
It is not a separate representation.

\subsection{Fidelity Has a Quality Price and a Transfer Cost}
\label{sec:obs-quality}

Figure~\ref{fig:bg-ppl} changes the fraction of experts stored at low precision.
Each point demotes a longer suffix of one fixed ranking, so the curve prices a fidelity quota.
The ranking stays frozen, and the sweep does not reassign which experts receive the higher-precision bytes.

WikiText-2 perplexity rises on both Qwen3-30B (FP16/INT4) and Qwen3-Next-80B (INT4/INT2) as more experts move to the lower tier.
The rise is continuous, so a quality-degradation limit selects how large the low-precision fraction may become.
The higher-precision representation is also the larger packed copy.
Raising the fidelity share therefore spends expert memory and increases the bytes a later transfer must move.
Which experts should receive those bytes is still open.

\subsection{The Experts a Workload Uses Also Change}
\label{sec:obs-demand}

The quality curve leaves the recipients unnamed.
Figure~\ref{fig:bg-hotsets} counts the token--expert dispatches at Layer~15 of Qwen3-30B on WikiText, GSM8K, and HumanEval.
Every expert stays at low precision, and the counters reset between workloads, so the histograms show which experts each workload uses.

The three tasks dispatch different experts.
A fidelity assignment or a resident set taken from one workload therefore misses experts the next workload uses.
The counts record access frequency.
They leave quantization sensitivity unmeasured, so they do not rank the quality harm of a low-precision expert.
Both the fidelity recipients and the resident set are workload-dependent inputs to the same allocation.

\subsection{Whether the Remaining Bytes Can Be Hidden Also Changes}
\label{sec:obs-overlap}

The quality curve prices the fidelity quota, and the dispatch counts show that the accessed experts change with the task.
What remains open is whether an expert that is still absent can arrive before it is used.
Figure~\ref{fig:bg-transfer-pressure} compares, for Qwen3-30B, the bytes selected in one MoE layer with an upper bound on the bytes that can move during that layer's compute.

Demand comes from five measured prompt blocks at each batch size.
The bound multiplies measured blocking host-to-device bandwidth by mean TTFT or TPOT and divides by 48 routed layers.
The bound is an optimistic ceiling: it ignores each expert's deadline and the bandwidth concurrent kernels take.
It is the capacity of the measured copy rate, not the result of an overlapping offload runtime.

Prefill touches most routed experts, and its compute window grows with the batch, so the overlap bound rises with demand.
Decode touches fewer experts, but its shorter window leaves less time before use.
The gap between selected bytes and this bound is the traffic lookahead cannot hide at the measured rate.
Prefill and decode move that gap in opposite directions, so one lookahead depth does not fit both phases.
Representation size stays fixed in this comparison.

\subsection{A Fidelity Change Moves Residency and Lookahead}
\label{sec:obs-joint}

Figure~\ref{fig:bg-joint} places fidelity, residency, and lookahead on one expert-memory budget at the measured host-to-device bandwidth of the replay.
The experiment replays a measured 2,048-token routing trace under the byte-accurate expert budget used by the Qwen3-30B runtime.
An illustrative WikiText-2 limit of 2.1 perplexity above the all-high configuration leaves only the 25\% and 40\% high-precision fractions feasible among the scanned shares.

Residents follow the frequency ranking from warm-up traces.
Lookahead has oracle knowledge of the measured active set, and its staging slots and the measured transfer window limit how many experts it can stage.
This replay is not an end-to-end \systemname\ result.

At a 25\% high-precision share, 12 staging slots leave 96 low-precision residents per layer and reduce exposed transfer to 0.44\,ms/request.
At a 40\% high-precision share, exposed transfer is minimized at eight slots with 43 low-precision residents, then rises because additional staging displaces residents.
Raising the high-precision share from 25\% to 40\% therefore changes both residency and the staging depth that reduces exposed transfer.
Host-to-device bandwidth stays at the measured rate of this trace.

\subsection{Motivation and Design Challenges}

The four measurements are the inputs of Eq.~\eqref{eq:objective}.
Quantization, caching, and prefetching can coexist in one system.
A fixed quota or a fixed priority cannot compare the opportunity cost of fidelity, residency, and lookahead as the workload changes.

\paragraph{Challenge 1: heterogeneous value.}
The three uses are measured in different units and last for different amounts of time.
Admission checks \(\widehat R(X)\le\widehat\epsilon\), and measured \(\Delta Q\) is reported against \(\epsilon\).
\(\widehat R\) is not converted into milliseconds or added to the latency objective.

\paragraph{Challenge 2: future-dependent value at different time scales.}
Routing locality and deadlines change faster than the quality evidence required to justify a fidelity transition.
The same allocation still assigns expert memory and transfer bandwidth, while the evidence and copy cost of fidelity take longer to amortize.

\paragraph{Challenge 3: hard memory safety during transitions.}
A final resident set that fits in \(B\) can still pass through a transition that does not.
Promotion and prefetch require destination bytes before a copy begins, and an old published representation remains occupied until its readers finish.
A representation still in transfer cannot execute.
