\section{Background and Motivation}
\label{sec:bg}

\subsection{Expert Memory on One Accelerator}
\label{sec:serving}

An MoE layer routes each token to \(k_{\ell}\) experts, and the router determines which experts execute.
Expert-choice routing lets each expert select its tokens~\cite{zhou2022expertchoice}.
That choice does not determine where their weights reside or when they arrive on the device.
A selected expert that is absent must be transferred from host memory, and a demand transfer exposes that time on the forward path.

Memory for non-expert parameters, the KV cache, activations, and fixed workspaces is reserved first, and the remaining capacity is the expert-memory budget \(B\).
Host memory stores a packed low-precision representation and a packed high-precision representation of every routed expert, with aggregate footprints \(P_{\mathrm{lo}}\) and \(P_{\mathrm{hi}}\).
We write \(\PhiRatio=B/P_{\mathrm{lo}}\) and \(\rho=P_{\mathrm{hi}}/P_{\mathrm{lo}}\).
At any instant an expert is absent or resident at low or high precision, and the forward pass can use only a published resident representation.
The host-to-device copy queue has measured rate \(C\).
The size of the copy, whether the expert stays on the GPU, and when the transfer occupies the queue decide which transfers consume this rate.

The same bytes in \(B\) pay for three things.
\emph{Fidelity} is the extra bytes of the high-precision copy.
\emph{Residency} keeps an expert on the GPU so a later use needs no copy.
\emph{Lookahead} starts that copy early enough to overlap earlier compute, and once the copy is published it is residency at the chosen tier.

\subsection{A Larger Copy Costs Perplexity and Bytes}
\label{sec:obs-quality}

Figure~\ref{fig:bg-ppl} changes the fraction of experts stored at low precision.
Each point demotes a longer suffix of one fixed ranking, so the curve is the perplexity cost of the low-precision fraction.
The ranking stays frozen, and the sweep does not reassign which experts receive the higher-precision bytes.

WikiText-2 perplexity rises on Qwen3-30B (FP16/INT4), Qwen3-Next-80B (INT4/INT2), and Phi-3.5-MoE (FP16/INT4) as more experts move to the lower tier. Each model is demoted along its own ranking.
The rise is continuous. Section~\ref{sec:obs-joint} sets each model's \(\epsilon\) to the smallest one-decimal number that covers that model's 25\% high-precision gap.
The higher-precision representation is also the larger packed copy.
Raising the fidelity share therefore spends expert memory and increases the bytes a later transfer must move.

The three tasks dispatch different experts.

\subsection{Whether the Remaining Bytes Can Be Hidden Also Changes}
\label{sec:obs-overlap}

Figure~\ref{fig:bg-transfer-pressure} asks whether an expert that is still absent can arrive before it is used.
It compares, for Qwen3-30B, the bytes selected in one MoE layer with an upper bound on the bytes that can move during that layer's compute.

Demand comes from five measured prompt blocks at each batch size.
The bound multiplies measured blocking host-to-device bandwidth by mean TTFT or TPOT and divides by 48 routed layers.
The bound is an optimistic ceiling: it ignores each expert's deadline and the bandwidth concurrent kernels take.

Prefill touches most routed experts, so selected bytes stay nearly flat while the overlap bound rises with the batch.
Decode selects fewer bytes, and those bytes grow with the batch while the overlap bound stays near the per-layer decode window.
In both panels the bound lies above selected bytes.
The slack between them widens with batch size in prefill and narrows in decode, so one lookahead depth does not fit both phases.
Representation size stays fixed in this comparison.

\subsection{A Larger High-Precision Share Changes Who Stays and How Far Ahead the Copy Starts}
\label{sec:obs-joint}

Figure~\ref{fig:bg-joint} places fidelity, residency, and lookahead on one expert-memory budget at the measured host-to-device bandwidth of the replay.
The experiment replays a measured 2,048-token routing trace under the byte-accurate expert budget used by the Qwen3-30B runtime.
Residents follow the frequency ranking from warm-up traces.
At a 25\% high-precision share, 12 staging slots leave 96 low-precision residents per layer and reduce exposed transfer to 0.44\,ms/request.
At a 40\% high-precision share, exposed transfer is minimized at eight slots with 43 low-precision residents, then rises because additional staging displaces residents.
Raising the high-precision share from 25\% to 40\% therefore changes both residency and the staging depth that reduces exposed transfer.
On Figure~\ref{fig:bg-ppl}(a) these two shares sit 0.62 and 0.40 above all-high, \(7.034-6.413\) and \(6.813-6.413\), and the 10\% share and the all-low point sit 0.76 and 0.78 above it, \(7.170-6.413\) and \(7.189-6.413\).
On Figure~\ref{fig:bg-ppl}(b) the same shares sit 0.54 and 0.38 above all-high, \(6.280-5.740\) and \(6.120-5.740\), and the 10\% share and the all-low point sit 0.62 and 0.66 above it, \(6.360-5.740\) and \(6.400-5.740\).
\(\epsilon\) is the smallest one-decimal number that is at least this 25\% gap, so \(\epsilon=0.70\) on Qwen3-30B and \(0.60\) on Qwen3-Next-80B.
On Figure~\ref{fig:bg-ppl}(c), the point with 75\% of experts at low precision is a 25\% high-precision share. It sits 0.83 above all-high, \(6.840-6.010\). The same rule gives \(\epsilon=0.90\). An operator fixes \(\epsilon\) with this rule before the evaluation prompts are read. A point \(0.02\) to \(0.09\) above \(\epsilon\) is outside Eq.~\eqref{eq:objective}. Section~\ref{sec:threats} gives the uncertainty of the downstream scores. Table~\ref{tab:eval-knobs} gives the same budgets inside tighter fixed bounds.
The raw 25\% gaps are not \(\epsilon\). Panel (b)'s 0.54 is the WikiText-2 gap \(6.280-5.740\). Table~\ref{tab:eval-main}'s \textsc{Static-Joint} \(\Delta\mathrm{PPL}\) is also 0.54, from the mixed-prompt values \(6.28-5.74\). Panel (a)'s 0.62 is the WikiText-2 gap. Table~\ref{tab:eval-main}'s 0.62 is \(7.03-6.41\) on the mixed prompts, and panel (a) ends at 7.19 rather than the 7.29 all-low value in Table~\ref{tab:eval-accuracy}. Setting \(\epsilon\) to either mixed-prompt value would place it on one of those rows. Phi-3.5-MoE's WikiText-2 gap is \(6.840-6.010=0.83\). Table~\ref{tab:eval-main}'s 0.83 is HOBBIT's mixed-prompt \(\Delta\mathrm{PPL}\). Phi-3.5-MoE's mixed-prompt all-low degradation is \(6.98-6.01=0.97\), above this ceiling.

\subsection{Motivation and Design Challenges}

The four measurements are the inputs of Eq.~\eqref{eq:objective}.
Quantization, caching, and prefetching can already run in one system, and the controller still has to decide which bytes to free.

\paragraph{Which spend is worth the bytes.}
A high-precision copy, a resident expert, and an early copy are measured in different units and last for different amounts of time.
\(\widehat R\) is not converted into milliseconds or added to the latency objective.

\paragraph{Which decision can wait.}
Routing and deadlines change faster than the perplexity evidence needed to justify a high-precision copy.
The same budget still assigns expert bytes and transfer time, while the evidence and the larger copy take longer to pay for themselves.

\paragraph{Which transition fits.}
A resident set that fits in \(B\) can still pass through a copy that does not.
A high-precision copy and an early copy need destination bytes before the transfer starts, the old copy stays occupied until its readers finish, and a copy still in transfer cannot execute.
